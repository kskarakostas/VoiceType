## Worker kill during processing: measured (Atlas, 2026-11-04)

Closes the L6 Deferred line "A worker killed during processing over 30 s". That line said the transcript is lost; for a worker left to idle, that is wrong.

Rig: Google Chrome 156.0.8078.79, headed in a nested X display, the 3.0.0 build unmodified, real X key input through `xdotool`, a TLS stub answering for `api.openai.com` and `generativelanguage.googleapis.com` through `--host-resolver-rules`. No DevTools attached. Chrome needs `--remote-debugging-pipe` to load an unpacked build, so one `Extensions.loadUnpacked` went down the pipe, and the pipe then sat idle with no target session. Control: with the extension idle the worker read "service worker (Inactive)" on `chrome://extensions` 45 s later, so nothing held it awake. Times are from the stop press, read from the stub's log.

- The transcript survives a 45 s provider: 5 of 5 on the shipped build, one of them a 180 s recording, one on Gemini. The worker was also alive at the 60 s request limit, since its own abort timer fired at 60.1 s. No idle kill was seen inside `REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS`.
- A worker that really dies during processing, which takes a crash, loses transcript and audio both, and the page reads "No response" at 75 s. The force-stop arm is also the proof that this rig can see a kill. History does not cover that case: no text exists yet.
- The pending reply to `OFFSCREEN_DONE` is not what keeps the worker alive (last row). What does was not traced; the likely candidate is the fetch in flight, ASSUMED, not tested.

Not measured: Chrome 140 to 155; a slow refine after a slow transcription; a laptop sleeping during processing.

Traps met on the way: Chrome's "Load unpacked" file dialog goes to the desktop portal, off the nested display, unless `DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS=disabled:` is set; a dialog pick there still loaded nothing. `Extensions.loadUnpacked` does not persist into the profile, so a plain restart has no extension. It needs `--remote-debugging-pipe`, not a port. `pgrep -f` or `pkill -f` on a pattern that appears in the calling shell's own command line kills that shell: bracket the pattern.
